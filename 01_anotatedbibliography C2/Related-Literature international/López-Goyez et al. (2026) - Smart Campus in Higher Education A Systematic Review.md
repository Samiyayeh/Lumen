# Smart Campus in Higher Education: A Systematic Review of Intelligent Technologies, Information Systems, and Sustainable Digital Ecosystems

**Category:** [[Related Literature international C2]]

**Juan P. López-Góyez, Alfonso González-Briones, and Jhonatan Guaytarilla. 2026. Smart Campus in Higher Education: A Systematic Review of Intelligent Technologies, Information Systems, and Sustainable Digital Ecosystems. *Applied Sciences* 16, 14 (2026), 7277. https://doi.org/10.3390/app16147277**

- **Summary:** This systematic review synthesizes 45 empirical and architectural smart campus studies across major scientific repositories (SpringerLink 35.6%, Scopus 28.9%, IEEE Xplore 20.0%, and ScienceDirect 15.6%) through the PRISMA framework and MMAT quality appraisal, identifying that 84.4% of implementations deploy multi-layered architectures converging IoT sensing, Big Data infrastructure, and predictive artificial intelligence models (such as CNN, LSTM, and GRU) to monitor institutional energy efficiency, space occupancy, and carbon neutrality initiatives.

- **Relevance:** This study directly validates the multi-tier architectural blueprint of the proposed system—specifically structuring the platform across perception (ESP32 CT sensors), network ingestion (MQTT), processing (Python backend), and centralized web presentation tiers (React dashboard)—while reinforcing the institutional necessity of automated energy informatics in higher education. Crucially, the review identifies two critical shortcomings in current higher education deployments: first, institutional energy initiatives remain heavily reliant on aggregate building-level metering or cost-prohibitive digital twin simulations that fail to deliver localized circuit-level granularity; second, existing smart campus architectures operate in administrative silos disconnected from institutional class timetables and lack ethical data governance frameworks. The proposed system directly resolves these operational voids by deploying non-invasive sub-panel CT clamps at the department level, pairing real-time power draw with an Isolation Forest algorithm cross-referenced against academic room schedules to flag unscheduled off-hours ghost consumption, and enforcing strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) compliant with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173).

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 3 & 3.1 (Results & RQ1: Intelligent Technologies and Digital Infrastructures, pp. 6–8):_ Breakdown of the 45-study corpus, documenting that 84.4% (38 studies) converge IoT and AI across layered perception, network, processing, and application architectures.
  - _Section 3.2 (RQ2: Architectures, Frameworks, or Operational Models, pp. 8–9):_ Analysis of cloud-centric vs. edge frameworks, highlighting recurring bottlenecks in latency, legacy system integration, and high capital expenditures.
  - _Section 3.3 & Section 5 (RQ3: Sustainability Dimensions & Discussion, pp. 9–11, 17–19):_ Documentation of the systemic failure of current smart campus research to integrate data governance and ethical privacy controls, alongside disconnected facility silos that lack operational integration with academic administrative schedules.
