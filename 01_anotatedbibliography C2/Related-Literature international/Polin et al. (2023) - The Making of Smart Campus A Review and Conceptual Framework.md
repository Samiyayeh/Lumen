# The Making of Smart Campus: A Review and Conceptual Framework

**Category:** [[Related Literature international C2]]

**Ken Polin, Tan Yigitcanlar, Mark Limb, and Tracy Washington. 2023. The Making of Smart Campus: A Review and Conceptual Framework. *Buildings* 13, 4 (2023), 891. DOI:https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings13040891**

- **Summary:** Polin et al. (2023) conducted a PRISMA-guided systematic literature review of peer-reviewed journal articles published between 2017 and 2022 indexed in Scopus, screening 3,601 initial candidate publications down to 123 qualifying studies to evaluate how smart campus initiatives are conceptualized and operationalized across four overarching domains: Economy (utility cost savings, smart infrastructure), Society (pedagogical environments, campus life), Environment (automated energy management, renewable integration, zero waste), and Governance (cybersecurity, decision-making, data governance); the synthesis revealed that more than 50% of smart campus publications remain purely conceptual with a marked scarcity of empirical hardware prototypes, while establishing automated energy monitoring and smart sub-metering as vital operational pillars for institutional sustainability.

- **Relevance:** This study directly informs and justifies the multi-tier architectural foundation of the proposed system by demonstrating that integrating IoT edge telemetry with centralized data governance and facility dashboards represents an established, essential requirement for institutional sustainability and utility cost containment in modern higher education institutions. However, the reviewed literature conceptualizes campus energy management almost exclusively at an aggregate, whole-building or macro-microgrid level (such as campus-wide central plants and high-level building automation systems), overlooking localized branch-circuit sub-metering. Furthermore, existing frameworks do not incorporate machine learning anomaly detection models (such as Isolation Forest) or context-aware mechanisms that cross-reference live electrical consumption against institutional class timetables and room occupancy schedules. Consequently, the synthesized literature leaves campus facility managers without actionable architectures to detect and isolate localized off-hours ghost consumption in unoccupied academic spaces. The proposed system resolves these conceptual and operational gaps by transitioning from theoretical smart campus taxonomies to a physical, empirical 3-tier IoT deployment: utilizing non-invasive ESP32 split-core CT clamp sub-metering inside departmental sub-panels, streaming telemetry via MQTT into a PostgreSQL time-series store, and executing context-aware Isolation Forest anomaly detection that actively correlates real-time power draw with campus academic schedules to automatically identify and flag unscheduled off-hours ghost loads on an interactive web dashboard.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 2.1 (Research Design, pp. 3–5):_ PRISMA methodology screening 3,601 Scopus articles (2017–2022) down to 123 core studies to formulate a smart campus taxonomy across economy, society, environment, and governance domains.
  - _Section 3.2.4 & 3.4 (Utility Cost Savings and Environment Domain, pp. 6–10):_ Classification of campus energy reduction and automated metering as core economic and environmental drivers, highlighting that 30 out of 40 environmental studies remain purely conceptual.
  - _Section 4 & 5 (Discussion and Conclusions, pp. 13–18):_ Review findings noting the lack of empirical physical deployments, reliance on macro-level models, and explicit recommendation for practical IoT-driven frameworks targeting granular resource optimization.
