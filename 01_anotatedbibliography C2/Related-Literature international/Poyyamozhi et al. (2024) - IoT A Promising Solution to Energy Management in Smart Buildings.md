# IoT—A Promising Solution to Energy Management in Smart Buildings: A Systematic Review, Applications, Barriers, and Future Scope

**Category:** [[Related Literature international C2]]

**Mukilan Poyyamozhi, Balasubramanian Murugesan, Narayanamoorthi Rajamanickam, Mohammad Shorfuzzaman, and Yasser Aboelmagd. 2024. IoT—A Promising Solution to Energy Management in Smart Buildings: A Systematic Review, Applications, Barriers, and Future Scope. *Buildings* 14, 11 (2024), 3446. https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings14113446**

- **Summary:** This systematic review analyzes IoT architectures and Building Energy Management Systems (BEMS) across hardware sensing, communication networking (MQTT, CoAP, Zigbee, and HTTP), and cloud analytics, evaluating empirical deployments in automated climate control, smart occupancy lighting, and virtual building automation that achieve up to 20% electrical energy reductions across commercial and institutional facilities.

- **Relevance:** This study directly substantiates the edge sensing and communication tiers of the proposed system, confirming that pairing non-invasive current sensing with lightweight publish-subscribe protocols (MQTT) establishes an energy-efficient, low-latency telemetry pipeline capable of overcoming the coverage and latency bottlenecks of legacy Building Automation Systems (BAS). Nevertheless, the review underscores critical operational vulnerabilities across current smart building literature: existing BEMS implementations are hindered by costly, invasive retrofitting requirements, proprietary protocol silos (e.g., conflicting Zigbee and BACnet standards), and heavy reliance on static rule-based thresholds that produce high false-alarm rates during occupancy shifts; moreover, existing systems lack contextual integration with institutional academic scheduling databases. The proposed system resolves these barriers by deploying non-invasive split-core CT clamps directly onto distribution panel branch circuits without disruptive structural rewiring, streaming telemetry over standardized MQTT to a PostgreSQL backend, and executing an unsupervised Isolation Forest algorithm cross-referenced against university class timetables to isolate unscheduled off-hours ghost loads.
