# A Survey on IoT-Enabled Smart Grids: Emerging, Applications, Challenges, and Outlook

**Category:** [[Related Literature international C2]]

**Arman Goudarzi, Farzad Ghayoor, Muhammad Waseem, Shah Fahad, and Issa Traore. 2022. A Survey on IoT-Enabled Smart Grids: Emerging, Applications, Challenges, and Outlook. *Energies* 15, 19 (2022), 6984. https://doi.org/10.3390/en15196984**

- **Summary:** This survey provides a taxonomy of IoT-driven energy networks structured across a four-layered architectural model—physical, communication network, cyber (decision-making), and application layers—while conducting an extensive cyber-physical vulnerability analysis of smart grid deployments, categorizing reconnaissance, false data injection (FDI), and denial-of-service attack vectors against Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI) and building energy systems, and evaluating defensive countermeasures including cryptographic payload encryption, distributed intrusion detection, and role-based access control.

- **Relevance:** This study provides direct theoretical and security justification for the architectural slicing and data governance of the proposed system: mapping the physical layer to ESP32 CT clamp sensors, the communication network layer to campus Wi-Fi telemetry pipelines, the cyber-decision layer to the Python backend with PostgreSQL and Isolation Forest anomaly detection, and the application layer to the React administrative dashboard. Crucially, the survey reinforces the necessity of embedding strict cyber-physical safeguards—specifically Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), encrypted payload transmission, and compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173)—to prevent false data injection, energy theft, and unauthorized manipulation of institutional consumption records. However, the survey reveals that existing smart grid literature is heavily skewed toward utility-scale SCADA networks, regional power transmission, and transactive market operations reliant on complex industrial computing infrastructure; the literature lacks lightweight, cost-effective institutional sub-metering architectures that correlate non-invasive branch circuit power draw with academic room schedules to identify localized off-hours ghost consumption.
