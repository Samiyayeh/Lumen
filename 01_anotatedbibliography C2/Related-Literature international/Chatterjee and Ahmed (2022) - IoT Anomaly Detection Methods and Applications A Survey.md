# IoT Anomaly Detection Methods and Applications: A Survey

**Category:** [[Related Literature international C2]]

**Ayan Chatterjee and Bestoun S. Ahmed. 2022. IoT Anomaly Detection Methods and Applications: A Survey. *Internet of Things* 19 (2022), 100568. DOI:https://doi.org/10.1016/j.iot.2022.100568**

- **Summary:** Chatterjee and Ahmed (2022) conducted a comprehensive state-of-the-art survey evaluating 64 qualifying research publications between January 2019 and July 2021 across diverse IoT anomaly detection domains (time-series sensor streaming, smart cities, machinery condition monitoring, network traffic, and physical infrastructure security); the authors formulated a 4-dimensional taxonomy classifying anomaly detection by method (geometrical, statistical, machine learning), by application (point, contextual, collective), by latency (online real-time versus offline batch), and by nature (constructive data cleaning and fault detection versus malicious intrusion detection), demonstrating that supervised models routinely fail in practical IoT deployments due to the severe scarcity of labeled ground-truth anomalies, and establishing unsupervised density and isolation techniques as the most viable paradigm for time-series sensor streams.

- **Relevance:** This study directly informs and justifies the machine learning and analytical tier of the proposed system by establishing that unsupervised isolation algorithms (specifically Isolation Forest) represent the optimal approach for continuous time-series IoT sensor streams because they isolate deviations in sparse feature spaces without requiring expensive, labor-intensive manual labeling or balanced anomaly training datasets. However, the survey reveals that existing IoT anomaly detection literature evaluates sensor telemetry in isolation from external operational schedules and institutional business context. Most surveyed models evaluate incoming values strictly against statistical historical baselines or static numerical thresholds, leaving them highly susceptible to concept drift caused by shifting semester routines. Crucially, none of the surveyed architectures integrate institutional class timetables or room occupancy schedules. Without schedule context, unsupervised models cannot determine whether an observed power spike represents legitimate off-hours instruction or unauthorized ghost consumption across campus distribution circuits. The proposed system resolves these survey-identified limitations by implementing an online Isolation Forest algorithm on a centralized Python backend that actively cross-references real-time electrical telemetry from non-invasive ESP32 CT sub-metering nodes directly against institutional academic class schedules, dynamically suppressing false alarms during scheduled classes and automatically isolating unauthorized off-hours ghost consumption.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 2.1 (Definition and Categorization of IoT Anomaly Detection, pp. 2–4):_ Formulation of the 4-dimensional taxonomy classifying IoT anomaly detection by method (geometrical, statistical, machine learning), latency (online vs. offline), application, and nature (constructive fault vs. malicious attack).
  - _Section 3 & 4.1 (Methodology & Time-Series Applications, pp. 5–9):_ Analysis of 64 qualifying papers (2019–2021), evaluating unsupervised reconstruction models and isolation techniques on streaming sensor telemetry.
  - _Section 5 (Conclusions and New Perspectives, pp. 15–16):_ Identification of open research challenges, highlighting the necessity for unsupervised online detection due to absent ground-truth data, resilience against concept drift, and multi-dimensional contextual data integration.
