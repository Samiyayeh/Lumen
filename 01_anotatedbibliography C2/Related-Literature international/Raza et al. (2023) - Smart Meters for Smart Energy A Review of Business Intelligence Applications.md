# Smart Meters for Smart Energy: A Review of Business Intelligence Applications

**Category:** [[Related Literature international C2]]

**Muhammad Haseeb Raza, Yousaf M. Rind, Isma Javed, Muhammad Zubair, Muhammad Qasim Mehmood, and Yehia Massoud. 2023. Smart Meters for Smart Energy: A Review of Business Intelligence Applications. *IEEE Access* 11 (2023), 120001–120022. DOI:https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3326724**

- **Summary:** Raza et al. (2023) conducted a comprehensive state-of-the-art review examining the convergence of smart meter telemetry, distributed Internet of Things (IoT) computing strata, and Business Intelligence (BI) applications in modern energy systems; the study synthesized architectural implementations across edge, fog, and cloud computational layers, mapping how raw high-frequency electrical telemetry (active, reactive, and apparent power, phase angle, and harmonics) is acquired by edge sensors, routed across communication protocol stacks (such as MQTT and HTTP), ingested into time-series data stores, and transformed into visual business intelligence dashboards to drive automated demand-response, predictive maintenance, load profiling, and anomaly detection.

- **Relevance:** This study directly informs and justifies the data-flow pipeline and frontend visualization tier of the proposed system by demonstrating that raw electrical telemetry alone produces operator data fatigue unless structured through multi-layer computational stacks into intuitive business intelligence dashboards featuring automated thresholding and anomaly indicators. However, the reviewed business intelligence frameworks focus heavily on macro-utility distribution grids, wholesale demand-response markets, and high-level residential billing, leaving institutional branch-level sub-metering underserved. Crucially, existing BI dashboards lack integration with institutional academic timetables and facility room reservation schedules. Without contextual schedule cross-referencing, facility managers cannot determine whether observed off-hours consumption represents legitimate academic activities or unauthorized ghost consumption, leaving localized energy waste unaddressed across departmental facilities. The proposed system directly resolves these operational boundaries by implementing an institutional-scale BI monitoring dashboard (React and Tailwind CSS) that pairs localized branch sub-metering telemetry (ESP32 CT clamps streamed via MQTT into PostgreSQL) with a centralized Isolation Forest analytics engine that actively cross-references live power draw against campus class schedules to automatically flag unscheduled off-hours ghost consumption and deliver actionable alerts to facility managers.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section II & III (Data Collection, Analytics, and Computational Stacks, pp. 120003–120010):_ Synthesis of multi-tier IoT smart meter architectures, mapping data flows across physical sensing, edge pre-processing, fog/cloud time-series storage, and analytical layers.
  - _Section VI.A–D (Smart Meter Implementations and Multi-Layer Applications, pp. 120013–120016):_ Analysis of protocol landscapes (MQTT, HTTP, CoAP), electrical parameters captured (active/reactive power, harmonics), and multi-layer operational roles.
  - _Section VII (Conclusion and Research Gaps, pp. 120017–120020):_ Identification of operational gaps, emphasizing that high-volume time-series telemetry induces operator fatigue unless coupled with automated, context-specific decision support and localized anomaly alerting.
