# Toward Delicate Anomaly Detection of Energy Consumption for Buildings: Enhance the Performance From Two Levels

**Category:** [[Related Study international C2]]

**Dong Wang, Therese Enlund, Johan Trygg, Mats Tysklind, and Lili Jiang. 2022. Toward Delicate Anomaly Detection of Energy Consumption for Buildings: Enhance the Performance From Two Levels. *IEEE Access* 10 (2022), 31649–31659. DOI:https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2022.3160170**

- **Summary:** Wang et al. (2022) developed and evaluated a two-level unsupervised anomaly detection framework (operating across the data structure level and algorithm mechanism level) evaluated against 8,760 hourly electricity consumption records across the full calendar year of 2018 from three commercial facilities in Sweden (retrieved from main electricity meters via Mestro AB): Dataset A (1,622 m² retail building in Karlskoga), Dataset B (47,166 m² university academic building in Göteborg), and Dataset C (28,046 m² retail building in Jönköping); the data structure level transforms raw univariate load profiles into contextualized multi-attribute feature matrices (incorporating temporal periodicities across month, day-of-week, hour, and working versus non-working days), while the algorithm level benchmarks four unsupervised outlier detectors (Local Outlier Factor [LOF], Connectivity-Based Outlier Factor [COF], Cluster-Based Local Outlier Factor [CBLOF], and Isolation Forest [IF]), demonstrating that contextualization improved Precision-Recall Area Under the Curve (AUC_PR) across all detectors, with LOF achieving peak AUC_PR scores of 0.989 (Dataset A), 0.941 (Dataset B), and 0.957 (Dataset C).

- **Relevance:** This study directly informs and justifies the machine learning and analytical tier of the proposed system by demonstrating that contextualizing electrical time-series data with operational calendar attributes substantially outperforms raw power thresholds, enabling unsupervised algorithms to accurately isolate subtle consumption anomalies concealed beneath baseline diurnal variations. However, the study relies entirely on retrospective, offline batch processing of historical annual smart meter datasets with a coarse hourly sampling frequency (1 record/hour) provided by an external utility aggregator (Mestro AB), entirely lacking an active IoT edge hardware tier (such as ESP32 microcontrollers paired with non-invasive split-core CT sensors). Furthermore, while the study includes an academic building testbed (Dataset B in Göteborg), its contextualization is limited to coarse calendar time variables (month, weekday, hour) without integrating institutional academic class timetables or room occupancy schedules, rendering it incapable of distinguishing scheduled off-hours instruction from unauthorized ghost energy consumption at the room or department sub-panel level. The proposed system resolves these boundaries by deploying high-frequency, non-invasive ESP32 CT sub-metering nodes inside department sub-panels for localized spatial granularity and implementing context-aware Isolation Forest anomaly detection that actively cross-references real-time electrical draw directly against campus academic class schedules to identify and flag unscheduled off-hours ghost consumption via an interactive web dashboard.

- **Source Reference(s):**

  - _Section IV & V (Methods and Workflow, pp. 31652–31654):_ Mathematical formulation of LOF, COF, CBLOF, and Isolation Forest detectors applied across original versus contextualized data matrices.
  - _Section VI (Results and Discussion, pp. 31654–31657):_ Empirical reporting of AUC_PR improvements across all contextualized models, led by LOF (0.989 for Dataset A, 0.941 for Dataset B, 0.957 for Dataset C).
  - _Section VII (Conclusion and Future Work, pp. 31657–31658):_ Explicit acknowledgment that the framework operates on retrospective commercial data and requires future work in real-time edge monitoring with higher sampling resolutions to pinpoint specific equipment faults.
