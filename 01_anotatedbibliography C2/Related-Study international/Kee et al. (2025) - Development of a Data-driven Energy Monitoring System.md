# Development of a Data-driven Energy Monitoring System for Power Consumption and Power Quality Monitoring

**Category:** [[Related Study international C2]]

**Owen Kwong-Hong Kee, Keh-Kim Kee, Ching Yee Yong, Ramli Rashidi, and Tsu Hsiung Lo. 2025. Development of a Data-driven Energy Monitoring System for Power Consumption and Power Quality Monitoring. *Journal of Renewable Energy and Smart Grid Technology* 20, 2 (2025), 163–175. DOI:https://doi.org/10.69650/rast.2025.263660**

- **Summary:** Kee et al. (2025) designed, developed, and validated an IoT-based multi-tier Data-Driven Energy Monitoring System (DDEMS, unit cost ~$120) consisting of an ESP32 edge microcontroller paired with PZEM-004T, ZMPT101B voltage transformer, and SCT-013 non-invasive split-core current transformer sensors executing real-time windowed Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) for harmonic analysis; data is transmitted via Wi-Fi HTTP POST requests with encapsulated JSON payloads to an Ubuntu 20.04 LTS VPS hosting Apache, PHP 8.1, and MySQL 8.0, where a cloud-based rule engine classifies power quality events across four severity levels based on IEEE 1159 and IEC 61000-4-30 standards, achieving an overall Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) of 1.24% when benchmarked against industrial Lovato DMG800 and Fluke 437-II analyzers over a 90-day field trial.

- **Relevance:** This study directly informs and justifies the hardware edge-sensing tier of the proposed system by demonstrating that an inexpensive ESP32 microcontroller paired with non-invasive split-core CT sensors (SCT-013) achieves high measurement fidelity (1.24% MAPE) comparable to multi-thousand-dollar commercial power analyzers ($5,000–$10,000) while executing on-chip edge pre-processing. However, DDEMS transmits telemetry via HTTP POST request-response transactions rather than lightweight publish-subscribe IoT streaming protocols (MQTT), increasing network payload overhead. Furthermore, its analytical layer is restricted to static, deterministic PHP rule thresholds focused on electrical grid power quality events (voltage sags, harmonics, power factor) in single-phase residential circuits, entirely lacking unsupervised machine learning algorithms (such as Isolation Forest) and lacking integration with institutional academic timetables or class schedules, rendering it incapable of identifying unscheduled off-hours ghost consumption across campus department sub-panels. The proposed system resolves these boundaries by replacing HTTP transactions with lightweight MQTT telemetry, deploying non-invasive CT sub-metering across institutional sub-panels, and implementing a Python backend featuring Isolation Forest anomaly detection cross-referenced against campus academic schedules to automatically identify and alert personnel to off-hours ghost energy waste.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 2.1 & 2.2 (System Architecture Overview & Hardware):_ Implementation of the 5-layer fog-cloud DDEMS framework coupling an ESP32 microcontroller with PZEM-004T, ZMPT101B, and SCT-013 current sensors to sample electrical parameters and perform windowed FFT.
  - _Section 2.4 & 2.5 (Web Portal & Rule-Based PQ Advisor):_ HTTP POST data transmission to an Apache/PHP 8.1 and MySQL 8.0 cloud backend on Ubuntu Server 20.04 LTS running deterministic rule-based event categorization under IEEE 1159 and IEC 61000-4-30.
  - _Section 3.2 (Cross-Verification with Reference Instruments):_ Experimental benchmarking against Lovato DMG800 and Fluke 437-II reference analyzers yielding an overall system MAPE of 1.24% at ~$120 unit cost.
  - _Section 4.1 (Limitations and Future Work):_ Explicit acknowledgment of single-phase residential limitations, lack of energy loss quantification, absence of predictive machine learning models, and need for standardized multi-phase industrial protocols.
