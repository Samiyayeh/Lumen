# Performance Analysis of MQTT and HTTP Protocols on Low-Power ESP32 Devices for IoT Applications

**Category:** [[Related Study international C2]]

**Syahrul Adriansyah, Sumarno, Ulya Mutiara Nurfuha, Abidzar Giffari, Tsulsi Khoirunisa, and Winky Gunsan Subarkah. 2026. Performance Analysis of MQTT and HTTP Protocols on Low-Power ESP32 Devices for IoT Applications. *CoreID Journal* 4, 1 (2026), 38–45. DOI:https://doi.org/10.60005/coreid.v4i1.160**

- **Summary:** Adriansyah et al. (2026) evaluated transport protocol efficiency on low-power ESP32 microcontrollers through a PRISMA 2020 Systematic Literature Review synthesizing empirical benchmarking studies; the investigation measured transmission latency, power consumption, protocol overhead, and packet loss across MQTT publish-subscribe streams and HTTP request-response architectures, finding that HTTP response times are 4.76× higher in cloud deployments and 12.1× higher in fog deployments under high-traffic conditions compared to MQTT, while MQTT delivers 6.03% (QoS 0) to 8.33% (QoS 1) energy savings, minimal 2-byte fixed header overhead, and superior delivery reliability over constrained wireless networks.

- **Relevance:** This study directly informs and justifies the transport protocol and hardware layer of the proposed system by providing empirical evidence that deploying lightweight MQTT publish-subscribe messaging over ESP32 edge microcontrollers drastically minimizes data transmission latency (up to 12.1× faster than HTTP) and reduces network protocol overhead, satisfying the system's ISO/IEC 25010 performance efficiency requirements. However, the study is strictly confined to transport-layer protocol evaluation and a literature review synthesis, lacking a physical sensor testbed for electrical power monitoring (such as split-core current transformer clamps), a persistent time-series database backend (PostgreSQL), automated machine learning anomaly detection (Isolation Forest), and integration with institutional room-scheduling data. Consequently, it cannot measure physical electrical variables (current, real power), isolate localized departmental circuits, or identify unscheduled off-hours ghost consumption across campus academic facilities. The proposed system resolves these boundaries by coupling the empirical transport advantages of MQTT with physical non-invasive CT clamp sub-metering deployed on ESP32 microcontrollers inside campus distribution panels, routing high-frequency telemetry into a PostgreSQL time-series store, and executing context-aware Isolation Forest algorithms cross-referenced against campus class schedules to detect and alert facility personnel to off-hours ghost energy waste.
