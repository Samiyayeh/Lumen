# A Low-Carbon Smart Campus Created by the Strategic Usage of Space—A Case Study of Korea University

**Category:** [[Related Study international C2]]

**Da Yeon Park and Mi Jeong Kim. 2025. A Low-Carbon Smart Campus Created by the Strategic Usage of Space—A Case Study of Korea University. *Buildings* 15, 12 (2025), 1972. DOI:https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings15121972**

- **Summary:** Park and Kim (2025) evaluated campus energy consumption characteristics, space usage behavior, and lecture room scheduling across 15 academic buildings in the humanities campus of Korea University using 10 years of institutional utility data (January 2011 to December 2020) and building energy simulation software (ECO2 modeling 102 zones in building B27); the investigation analyzed correlations between physical building attributes (floor area, completion date, building type) and human-centered lecture timetables, demonstrating that spatial classroom consolidation and timetable adjustments (such as rescheduling underutilized Friday lecture blocks) increased room occupancy rates by 26.19% to 41.7%, reduced HVAC operating duration by 21.3% to 43.5%, and reduced cooling energy consumption by 15.9% to 18.6% (yielding 4.0% to 5.3% overall energy savings and 4.7% to 6.2% greenhouse gas reductions).

- **Relevance:** This study directly informs and justifies the context-aware scheduling and ghost load auditing module of the proposed system by empirically proving that campus energy consumption is strongly governed by academic timetables rather than ambient weather alone, and confirming that cross-referencing facility operations against lecture schedules uncovers substantial avoidable energy waste during off-hours and low-occupancy periods. However, the study relies entirely on retrospective macro-utility billing logs, manual academic calendar audits, and offline software simulations (ECO2), entirely lacking an active IoT edge sensing infrastructure. It deploys no branch-circuit CT sensors or microcontrollers (ESP32) on sub-panels, possesses no live telemetry data pipelines (MQTT/PostgreSQL), and incorporates no automated machine learning anomaly detection (Isolation Forest). Consequently, it cannot capture localized, real-time power draw at the department or room level, detect unauthorized off-hours ghost consumption dynamically, or issue automated alerts to facility personnel through a live web monitoring dashboard. The proposed system directly resolves these operational boundaries by implementing non-invasive ESP32 CT sub-metering nodes deployed inside branch sub-panels to capture real-time electrical draw, coupling this live telemetry with a backend Isolation Forest engine that continuously cross-references live consumption against institutional class schedules to detect and alert facility managers to unscheduled off-hours ghost loads via an interactive React/Tailwind web dashboard.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 3 & 4 (Research Methodology & Case Study, pp. 3–8):_ Analysis of 10-year empirical energy usage logs (2011–2020) across 15 academic buildings at Korea University, categorizing spaces by physical parameters and academic timetable utilization.
  - _Section 5 (Simulating Energy Savings by Space Usage, pp. 8–11):_ ECO2 simulation across 102 zones of building B27 showing 26.19%–41.7% occupancy improvements, 21.3%–43.5% HVAC run-time reductions, and 15.9%–18.6% cooling energy cuts (4.0%–5.3% total building energy reduction).
  - _Section 6 & 7 (Discussion and Conclusions, pp. 11–13):_ Core findings highlighting high unoccupied off-hours and vacation energy consumption, and authors' explicit recommendation for automated, real-time monitoring platforms that zone and track spaces dynamically according to operational schedules.
