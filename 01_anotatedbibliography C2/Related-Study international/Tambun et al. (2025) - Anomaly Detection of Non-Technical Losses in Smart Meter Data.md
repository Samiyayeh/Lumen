# Anomaly Detection of Non-Technical Losses in Smart Meter Data Using K-Means Clustering and Isolation Forest: A Case Study From Indonesia

**Category:** [[Related Study international C2]]

**Rejeki Tambun, Ardyono Priyadi, and Vita Lystianingrum. 2025. Anomaly Detection of Non-Technical Losses in Smart Meter Data Using K-Means Clustering and Isolation Forest: A Case Study From Indonesia. In *2025 26th International Seminar on Intelligent Technology and Its Applications (ISITIA)*. IEEE, 1–6. DOI:https://doi.org/10.1109/ISITIA66279.2025.11137434**

- **Summary:** Tambun, Priyadi, and Lystianingrum (2025) evaluated an unsupervised anomaly detection framework designed to detect non-technical losses (NTL) and irregular electrical draw across voltage and current datasets from Indonesia's National Electricity Utility (PLN) spanning 2021 to 2023; focusing on commercial and industrial customers equipped with current transformer (CT)-based smart meters, the authors implemented data preprocessing, hyperparameter optimization, and comparative evaluation in the KNIME Analytics Platform, demonstrating that while K-Means clustering achieved up to 98.53% recall primarily on voltage anomalies (over-voltage and voltage drop), a tuned Isolation Forest model achieved up to 91.54% recall in identifying abnormal current patterns indicative of load bypasses and unauthorized consumption.

- **Relevance:** This study directly informs and justifies the machine learning backend tier of the proposed system by demonstrating that an Isolation Forest algorithm achieves high empirical recall (91.54%) in isolating unusual current flows and unmetered electrical deviations from CT-based telemetry without requiring labeled ground truth. However, the study confines its pipeline to offline desktop batch simulations within the KNIME Analytics Platform using historical utility smart meter datasets, entirely lacking an active IoT hardware sensing architecture (such as edge ESP32 microcontrollers streaming over lightweight MQTT). Furthermore, it focuses on utility-scale macro-distribution and revenue loss without department or room sub-panel disaggregation, and operates in isolation from institutional academic class schedules or room occupancy timetables, leaving it incapable of distinguishing scheduled operational activities from unauthorized off-hours ghost consumption. The proposed system resolves these boundaries by implementing an end-to-end, real-time IoT architecture combining low-cost ESP32 CT sub-metering nodes deployed inside campus distribution sub-panels with a centralized Python backend; this backend runs an online Isolation Forest algorithm continuously cross-referenced against institutional academic class schedules to detect and alert facility managers to off-hours ghost energy waste via an interactive React/Tailwind web dashboard.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section I & III (Introduction & Methodology, pp. 1–3):_ Formulation of the NTL anomaly detection workflow within the KNIME Analytics Platform, coupling K-Means clustering with the Isolation Forest algorithm on utility CT-meter voltage and current datasets (2021–2023).
  - _Section IV (Results and Discussion, pp. 3–5):_ Empirical comparison demonstrating that K-Means achieved 98.53% recall on voltage irregularities, while hyperparameter-tuned Isolation Forest achieved 91.54% recall on unusual current flows and load bypass events.
  - _Section V (Conclusion, pp. 5–6):_ Authors' conclusion validating the efficacy of unsupervised machine learning for electrical anomaly detection, noting the operational necessity of transitioning from offline utility simulations to real-time automated monitoring systems.
