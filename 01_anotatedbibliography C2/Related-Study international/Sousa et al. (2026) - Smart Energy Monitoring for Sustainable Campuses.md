# Smart Energy Monitoring for Sustainable Campuses: A Hybrid Anomaly Detection Approach Based on Prophet and Isolation Forest

**Category:** [[Related Study international C2]]

**Ângelo Sousa, Pedro J. S. Cardoso, and Jânio Monteiro. 2026. Smart Energy Monitoring for Sustainable Campuses: A Hybrid Anomaly Detection Approach Based on Prophet and Isolation Forest. *Sustainability* 18, 5 (2026), 2589. DOI:https://doi.org/10.3390/su18052589**

- **Summary:** Sousa et al. (2026) developed and evaluated a campus-scale electricity monitoring and analytical platform deployed across university facilities at the University of Algarve (pilot Building 1, Gambelas campus) that integrates heterogeneous Modbus RTU/TCP energy meters and SolarLog PV REST APIs with PostgreSQL storage, EmonCMS and Grafana operational dashboards, and a Python machine learning backend combining Prophet short-term forecasting (24-hour horizon at 15-minute intervals tuned via Mango Bayesian optimization) with an Isolation Forest detector (200 trees, 1% contamination factor) intersecting a 12-hour sliding-window Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) residual persistence rule (99.5th percentile threshold) to identify anomalous consumption spikes across 33 rolling-origin temporal cutoffs with approximately 95% forecast coverage.

- **Relevance:** This study directly informs and justifies the machine learning and analytical tier of the proposed system by empirically validating that pairing time-series forecast residuals with an Isolation Forest algorithm reliably isolates electrical consumption anomalies without requiring labeled training datasets. However, the system relies on macro-level building energy analysers and Modbus RTU/TCP gateways rather than localized, non-invasive split-core CT clamp microcontrollers (ESP32) inside sub-panels to isolate departmental and room-level power draw. Furthermore, its anomaly detection pipeline evaluates telemetry strictly against historical load profiles and weather covariates (Open-Meteo API) without cross-referencing real-time power draw against institutional academic timetables or room occupancy schedules, rendering it incapable of distinguishing scheduled off-hours academic activities from unauthorized ghost energy waste. The proposed system directly resolves these limitations by deploying low-cost ESP32 CT sub-metering nodes for department-level spatial granularity and implementing context-aware Isolation Forest auditing that cross-references live electrical draw directly against campus class schedules to identify and flag unscheduled off-hours ghost consumption.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 3.1 (System Architecture):_ Integration of Modbus RTU field meters, Modbus TCP gateways, SolarLog PV REST APIs, EmonCMS operational dashboard, and PostgreSQL database on virtualized campus infrastructure.
  - _Section 3.2.1 & 3.2.2 (Methodology - Forecasting & Anomaly Detection):_ Configuration of Prophet forecasting across 33 rolling cutoffs tuned via Mango Bayesian optimization, paired with a hybrid detector combining a 48-sample (12-hour) MAD residual persistence rule (99.5th percentile threshold) and an Isolation Forest model (200 trees, 0.01 contamination factor).
  - _Section 4.2 & 4.3 (Results & Validation):_ Reporting of ~95% forecast coverage, daily composite scores (5.61–10.23), and detection of persistent excess consumption spikes on September 15 and 18, 2025.
  - _Section 5 (Discussion & Limitations):_ Stated boundaries including reliance on external public weather APIs, manual tariff updates, single pilot facility validation, and explicit acknowledgment of the absence of real-time schedule and occupancy integration.
