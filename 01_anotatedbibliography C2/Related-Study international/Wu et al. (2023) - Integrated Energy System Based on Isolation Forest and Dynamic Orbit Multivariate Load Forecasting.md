# Integrated Energy System Based on Isolation Forest and Dynamic Orbit Multivariate Load Forecasting

**Category:** [[Related Study international C2]]

**Shidong Wu, Hengrui Ma, Abdullah M. Alharbi, Bo Wang, Li Xiong, Suxun Zhu, Lidong Qin, and Gangfei Wang. 2023. Integrated Energy System Based on Isolation Forest and Dynamic Orbit Multivariate Load Forecasting. *Sustainability* 15, 20 (2023), 15029. DOI:https://doi.org/10.3390/su152015029**

- **Summary:** Wu et al. (2023) developed and evaluated a multi-load forecasting and outlier filtering framework implemented in Python (TensorFlow and Keras running on an Intel Xeon4213R CPU and NVIDIA RTX 3080 GPU) evaluated against 46,000 operational records across 22 statistical features sampled at 15-minute intervals from the Tempe campus of Arizona State University (encompassing 288 buildings, >50,000 occupants, CCHP, and electric boilers from January 1, 2022 to May 1, 2023); the methodology constructs high-dimensional sliding time-window matrices to isolate outliers using an unsupervised Isolation Forest (iForest), repairs time-series sequences via a dynamic orbit channel, and feeds the sanitized data into a Temporal Convolutional Network Multi-gate Mixture-of-Learners (TCN-MMoL) deep architecture, reducing Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) across cooling, heating, and electric loads by 2.57%, 0.11%, and 0.49% respectively.

- **Relevance:** This study directly justifies the backend machine learning tier of the proposed system by demonstrating that applying an Isolation Forest algorithm to high-dimensional sliding-window energy matrices provides rapid, unsupervised isolation of anomalous electrical telemetry without requiring labeled ground truth. However, the study confines the Isolation Forest strictly to an offline, static data-scrubbing pre-processor to cleanse historical datasets for deep neural network training, entirely lacking real-time edge telemetry ingestion, automated web-based alerting, and localized sub-panel circuit disaggregation. Furthermore, it models aggregated macro-campus load profiles alongside external meteorological covariates without cross-referencing power draw against institutional academic class timetables or room occupancy schedules, leaving it incapable of identifying unscheduled off-hours ghost consumption in unoccupied academic facilities. The proposed system resolves these boundaries by operating Isolation Forest as an active, online surveillance engine within a lightweight Python backend, continuously cross-referencing real-time telemetry from non-invasive ESP32 CT sub-metering nodes directly against institutional academic class schedules to detect and alert facility personnel to off-hours ghost energy waste.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 3.1 & 3.3 (Data Feature Preprocessing):_ Construction of high-dimensional sliding-window matrices and formulation of the unsupervised Isolation Forest algorithm for outlier recognition across multi-energy load sequences.
  - _Section 5.1 & 5.2 (Experiment Description & Necessity of Global iForest):_ Empirical validation using 46,000 operational records across 22 features (15-minute granularity) from the 288-building Arizona State University Tempe campus, demonstrating iForest elimination of extreme order-of-magnitude anomalies.
  - _Section 5.4.1 (Validity Analysis of Preprocessing):_ Empirical reporting of MAPE reductions for cooling (2.57%), heating (0.11%), and electric load (0.49%) using the IDTMMoL model.
  - _Section 6 (Conclusions & Stated Boundaries):_ Summary of findings and explicit acknowledgment that the framework is limited to offline load, PV, and price forecasting without live edge IoT alerting or facility occupancy/schedule context.
