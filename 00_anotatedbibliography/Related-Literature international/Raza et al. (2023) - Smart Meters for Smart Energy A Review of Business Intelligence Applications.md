# Smart Meters for Smart Energy: A Review of Business Intelligence Applications

**Category:** [[Related Literature international]]

**Muhammad Haseeb Raza, Yousaf M. Rind, Isma Javed, Muhammad Zubair, Muhammad Qasim Mehmood, and Yehia Massoud. 2023. Smart Meters for Smart Energy: A Review of Business Intelligence Applications. *IEEE Access* 11 (2023), 120001–120022. https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3326724**

- **Summary:** Raza et al. review the convergence of smart meter telemetry and business intelligence analytics, highlighting how real-time consumption dashboards and automated data-driven alerts transform raw energy measurements into actionable insights for facility administrators.

- **Relevance:** The literature establishes that raw sub-metering data requires automated analytics and visual reporting pipelines to effectively influence institutional energy conservation, directly guiding the frontend dashboard design and alert generation pipelines in the proposed system.

- **Gap:** While the review demonstrates how business intelligence dashboards convert smart meter telemetry into energy conservation insights, existing literature focuses on macro-utility billing and grid-level distribution. Specifically, current business intelligence frameworks do not link circuit-level sub-metering with institutional room schedules. Without schedule cross-referencing, facility managers experience data fatigue from raw electrical measurements, leaving unauthorized off-hours ghost consumption unflagged across departmental academic facilities.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 2 (Smart Metering Infrastructure) & Section 3 (Data Analytics Pipelines, pp. 120003–120010):_ Synthesizes data flow architectures from IoT meters through time-series storage to real-time visual analytics.
  - _Section 4 (Applications in Institutional Facilities):_ Evaluates dashboard usability and automated notification mechanisms in driving operational energy savings.
  - _Section 5 (Research Gaps and Future Directions, pp. 120017–120019):_ Outlines the operational barrier where high-frequency telemetry causes operator fatigue unless paired with automated, context-specific decision support.
