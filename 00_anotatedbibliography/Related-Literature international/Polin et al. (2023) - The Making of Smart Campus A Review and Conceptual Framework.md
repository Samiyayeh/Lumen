# The Making of Smart Campus: A Review and Conceptual Framework

**Category:** [[Related Literature international]]

**Ken Polin, Tan Yigitcanlar, Mark Limb, and Tracy Washington. 2023. The Making of Smart Campus: A Review and Conceptual Framework. *Buildings* 13, 4 (2023), 891. https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings13040891**

- **Summary:** Polin et al. conduct a systematic review of international smart campus initiatives, synthesizing literature across governance, technology, and environmental domains to establish a conceptual framework for automated infrastructure and energy management in higher education.

- **Relevance:** The architectural taxonomy formulated in the study directly supports the system design of the proposed system, demonstrating that combining IoT sensor nodes with centralized energy monitoring dashboards provides the necessary operational visibility to curb institutional power waste.

- **Gap:** While the study formulates a taxonomy across smart campus domains, the synthesized literature conceptualizes energy management primarily at an aggregate, building-wide level. Specifically, existing frameworks do not incorporate circuit-level sub-metering or context-aware anomaly detection models that cross-reference live electrical draw against academic timetables. This theoretical focus leaves campus administrators without operational models to detect and isolate localized off-hours ghost consumption in unoccupied academic spaces.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 2 (Smart Campus Domains) & Section 3.3 (Environmental Domain, pp. 10–12):_ Classifies energy management and automated metering as core pillars, noting existing models primarily focus on building-level energy management systems (BEMS) and aggregate loads.
  - _Section 4 (Technological Infrastructure, pp. 14–17):_ Identifies the multi-tier integration of IoT edge sensors, telemetry communication protocols, and centralized analytical dashboards.
  - _Section 5 (Discussion and Conclusion, p. 18):_ Highlights the gap between high-level smart campus conceptualization and practical, empirical deployments across institutional settings.
