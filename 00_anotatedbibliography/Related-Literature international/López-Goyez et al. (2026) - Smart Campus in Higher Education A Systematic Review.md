# Smart Campus in Higher Education: A Systematic Review of Intelligent Technologies, Information Systems, and Sustainable Digital Ecosystems

**Category:** [[Related Literature international]]

**Juan P. López-Góyez, Alfonso González-Briones, and Jhonatan Guaytarilla. 2026. Smart Campus in Higher Education: A Systematic Review of Intelligent Technologies, Information Systems, and Sustainable Digital Ecosystems. *Applied Sciences* 16, 14 (2026), 7277. https://doi.org/10.3390/app16147277**

- **Summary:** López-Góyez et al. conduct a systematic review of smart campus implementations worldwide, analyzing how IoT telemetry, AI analytics, and centralized information dashboards drive environmental sustainability and institutional electricity conservation.

- **Relevance:** The review demonstrates that deploying sensor-driven sub-metering infrastructure combined with automated data dashboards is critical for educational institutions seeking to eliminate campus energy waste, providing architectural validation for the proposed system.

- **Gap:** While the systematic review synthesizes global smart campus implementations utilizing IoT and analytics, the literature reveals a persistent integration barrier: existing deployments rely on macro-building metering rather than granular sub-circuit telemetry. Specifically, current smart campus frameworks operate in isolation from institutional academic scheduling databases. Without timetable integration, facility managers cannot verify whether off-hours electrical draw reflects authorized academic activities or idle ghost consumption.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 3 (Intelligent Technologies in Campus Ecosystems):_ Evaluates the scalability and integration of IoT sensor networks, time-series telemetry pipelines, and centralized web interfaces across 45 analyzed smart campus studies.
  - _Section 4 (Environmental Sustainability and Energy Efficiency):_ Synthesizes evidence showing electricity conservation through load tracking while noting reliance on aggregate building-level monitoring.
  - _Section 5 & Section 6 (Challenges, Interoperability, and Future Directions):_ Documents system integration and data governance bottlenecks, emphasizing that campus energy management systems remain disconnected from academic administrative databases.
